# Shopfront Studio — Design Direction

## Three Visual Approaches Considered

| Theme Name | Very Brief Intro | Probability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Market Signal | A sharp retail-signage system that feels made for shop windows, weekly specials, and busy main streets. It makes the studio look direct, energetic, and commercially minded. | 0.07 |
| The Local Ledger | An editorial, newspaper-inspired studio identity that treats local commerce as cultural infrastructure. It would feel thoughtful, tactile, and quietly authoritative. | 0.03 |
| The Neon Checkout | A dark, kinetic world of high-saturation color and digital displays. It would position the studio as an experimental performance-marketing partner. | 0.09 |

## Chosen Approach: The Market Signal

### Design Movement

The site adopts **Swiss retail wayfinding meets contemporary editorial art direction**. It borrows the clarity of supermarket aisle markers, price stickers, window vinyl, and punchy circulars, but renders those references with a deliberate studio-level finish.

### Core Principles

1. **Signal before decoration:** Each element should answer a visitor's next question, as clearly as a shelf label helps a shopper choose.
2. **Bold commerce, human scale:** The brand is confident and punchy without becoming impersonal or corporate.
3. **Asymmetric rhythm:** Content moves through bands, columns, cropped cards, and visual interruptions rather than a standard centered landing-page stack.
4. **Tactile utility:** Marker strokes, ruled edges, perforation-style details, and sticker forms add a practical, printed-shop-floor feel.

### Color Philosophy

The visual system pairs a near-black **Ink** foundation with a warm paper-like **Counter Cream**, a vivid **Tangerine** signal color, and a pale **Citrus Lime** highlight. Ink establishes professional confidence; Counter Cream provides breathing room and a printed-retail texture; Tangerine delivers action and sales energy; Citrus Lime is used in small high-attention cues such as tags, counts, and “open” indicators. The ownable color is **Signal Tangerine (#FF5A36)**.

### Layout Paradigm

The page follows an **aisle-map composition**. A slim, persistent information rail gives visitors a visual anchor, while the main content alternates between oversized editorial spreads, service tickets, vertical markers, and compact promotional cards. The hero is an offset two-column split rather than a centered billboard.

### Signature Elements

1. **Price-marker circles** carrying simple numbers, arrows, or short status words.
2. **Print-ticket edges** and thin black rules that carve sections into commerce-like modules.
3. **The shop window frame**, a warm square outline that crops visual work and repeats as a brand device.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like placing a bright window sticker or selecting an aisle marker: crisp, visible, and decisive. Buttons compress slightly on press, navigation anchors glide smoothly to their section, and service cards change background rather than merely changing a border.

### Animation

The primary movement is a restrained reveal from 12px below with opacity for sections entering the viewport, staggered by 50ms across related cards. Price markers may rotate a few degrees into place once only. Hover effects use 160–220ms custom ease-out transitions and only opacity or transform. Motion is disabled for users who prefer reduced motion.

### Typography System

**Space Grotesk** is used for utilitarian, compact navigation and all-caps labels; **DM Sans** provides highly legible supporting copy; and **Archivo Black** carries the promotional headline voice. Headlines are large, mostly left-aligned, tightly tracked, and often broken across intentional lines. Body copy is comfortable and compact, avoiding excessive text width.

### Brand Essence

**Shopfront Studio builds storefront-ready websites and sharp advertising for independent shops and growing marts that want to stay visible where local buying happens.**

The brand is **direct, energetic, and street-smart**.

### Brand Voice

Headlines speak in retail language: short, specific, and action-oriented. CTAs are invitations to take a practical next step, not generic growth promises. Microcopy is concise and reassuring.

> “Make your storefront impossible to scroll past.”

> “Bring the weekday rush to your digital door.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The wordmark concept is a compact “S” formed by two offset price-ticket shapes, paired with a heavy bespoke-style SHOPFRONT lettering treatment. The symbol is designed to stand alone as a favicon, social avatar, and navigation mark; no text is embedded inside the mark.

### Style Decisions

The experience will use a warm light primary canvas rather than a dark interface, reserving the Ink background for high-impact moments. Rounded corners remain minimal; ticket cuts, sharp rules, circles, and offset shadows carry the personality instead. Visuals must reinforce local retail, not generic software aesthetics.

The price-marker circles carry functional retail information such as a number, a role, a status, or a clear directional action. Display emphasis follows retail-signage logic: heavy SHOPFRONT-style display type leads, with limited highlighted terms as marked-up commercial accents. The ticket “S” symbol recurs in headers, footers, labels, and key section markers to act as a recognizable studio stamp.

Cream-background sections retain a visible retail-system cue, such as a ruled ticket frame, an aisle label, a price marker, a shop-window crop, or a utility tag. Visual references remain grounded in street-level commerce: storefronts, shelves, counters, produce, window signage, and local shopping moments.

## Integrated 3D Scene Redesign

The hero is no longer treated as copy beside an image with added objects. It becomes a **scroll-led 3D retail display**: an atmospheric shop-window sculpture made of a deep Ink display bay, orange lacquered price rails, a lime acrylic offer disc, a paper-cream product plinth, and a real storefront-lighting mood. These pieces share one physical space, so they feel like a carefully built window display rather than floating graphic stickers.

The desktop experience will hold this scene in a sticky stage while the visitor scrolls through it. In the opening movement, the camera feels close to the display surface. The middle movement moves through the window and reveals the studio promise in layered stages. The exit movement softens the scene into the existing ticker and editorial content. The mobile version preserves the scene and progressive copy reveal but uses a shorter scroll distance and lighter effects to keep interaction smooth. The scene must support—not obstruct—the headline, CTA, and essential contrast.

Every scroll beat carries either a shop-window display cue or practical retail-wayfinding copy; no extended plain dark spacer is allowed. The final beat resolves into Counter Cream before the page continues, preserving Cream as the dominant canvas beyond the display bay. The Shopfront ticket “S” appears in each beat as an intentionally repeated studio stamp.

Signal Tangerine owns the large commercial gestures, while Citrus Lime is reserved for high-attention offer discs, counts, status markers, and small callouts. Each major page zone carries a retail-system cue such as an aisle rail, a ticket stamp, a marker, a storefront crop, or a ruled module, so the visitor experiences one continuous shopfront route rather than a sequence of unrelated panels.

Monocraft is used as the studio’s digital-signage voice for navigation, labels, price markers, interaction controls, and wayfinding copy. The display face remains reserved for major commercial headlines and the clean body face stays in place for longer reading and form completion.

## Style Decisions

- Pixel typography is reserved for signage accents, selected commercial words, labels, counts, controls, and wayfinding. Primary promotional headlines retain the heavy Shopfront-style display face.
- Counter Cream sections must carry an active retail-system cue such as a ruled route, ticket frame, price marker, aisle label, or stamped symbol. The black featured-system panel remains intentionally image-free after the Work introduction is removed, using a large Shopfront stamp and display rails instead.
